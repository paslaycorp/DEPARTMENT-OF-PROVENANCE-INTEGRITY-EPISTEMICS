# Genesis 2.0 vs. AEIF

## Purpose

This document records a provenance-first comparison between publicly described Genesis 2.0 material and the Alexandrian Epistemic Integrity Framework (AEIF).

It is a comparative record, not an accusation of copying or a determination of authorship or priority.

The comparison follows this sequence:

**similarity → correspondence → evidence → chronology → provenance**

Technical similarity alone does not establish copying, derivation, or priority.

## Scope

The comparison is limited to claims that can be tied to identifiable artifacts, public statements, specifications, implementations, commits, or other evidence. Where evidence is absent, conflicting, inaccessible, or insufficient, the claim remains unresolved rather than being completed by inference.

## Chronology

Chronology must be established from dated primary artifacts, repository history, publication records, or other independently verifiable evidence. A statement that two systems contain similar mechanisms does not establish which system preceded the other.

Where a precise date cannot be established from the available evidence, the date should remain PENDING or UNRESOLVED.

## Provenance rule

The following inference is prohibited:

> technical similarity → copying

The admissible progression is:

> similarity → correspondence → evidence → chronology → provenance

Even strong correspondence may establish only that two systems solve related problems in related ways. A claim of copying or derivation requires additional provenance evidence.

## Evidence discipline

For each material comparison, the repository's canonical provenance relation should be used:

**Claim → Artifact → Version → Date → Evidence → Hash → Location**

The comparison must preserve contradictory evidence and missing evidence. Absence of evidence shall not be interpreted as evidence of priority, derivation, or copying.
